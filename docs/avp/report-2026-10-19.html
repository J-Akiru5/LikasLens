The headline is that the screen recording is real: every AI result in the finished video came out of the running system during the take, not out of a script. That is also why the video differs from the storyboard in three places — the brief told the agent to say only what the screen showed, and the screen did not always oblige.

Revision after the independent QA pass

The first cut was reviewed by a separate agent that did not build it, and it found real defects. All have been fixed, and the video was re-recorded and re-assembled:

  • A rebuild blocker. A bad merge had reverted lib/config.mjs to the old geometry, so frame-overlay.mjs threw does not provide an export named TEXT_COLUMN on a clean clone. Restored.
  • Three narration claims the screens did not support — the location tagging itself, "late ones show as escalations", and "respond faster". All three were removed rather than faked.
  • The Liksi answer on screen was legally wrong — and was not Gemini at all. It is a hardcoded fallback in the mobile chat route, and it applies the ₱1,000,000 landfill ceiling to open burning and dumping. Scene 4 no longer asks a legal question.
  • The stored audio timing for scene 3 disagreed with the audio. synthesize.mjs now writes measured durations back into steps.json, so that drift cannot recur.

This is why the closing line is now captioned, why scene 5b is no longer silent, and why the report below records what the QA pass could not verify rather than asserting it was fine.

Spike results

Both spikes overturned the brief's assumption

S1 — can scenes 2–6 run with no write to the live Supabase project?

The brief's approach could not work, for a reason visible only in the code.

Section 6 said to fake Supabase with Playwright page.route. That only intercepts the browser. Both apps re-verify the session in the Next.js server — apps/mobile-pwa/src/proxy.ts:74 and apps/admin-portal/src/proxy.ts:51 — so the middleware would redirect every protected route to /login. Two further paths are equally unreachable: the app's own /api/v1/ai/reports route writing to Supabase with the service-role key, and the admin route handlers.

The replacement is stub/supabase-stub.mjs, a local server implementing enough of the Supabase wire protocol — auth, a PostgREST subset including RPC, and storage — that both the browser and the Next server can talk to it. The real apps run unmodified. This is exactly what the throwaway branch is for: "env wiring, fixture hooks, stubbed auth".

Also found while building S1

  • The brief's camera flag, --use-fake-device-with-video-capture, does not exist in Chromium. With it, getUserMedia finds no video track at all and the report page can never take a photo. The working flag is --use-fake-device-for-media-stream.
  • The report page has a real "02 AI Triage" step rendering an "AI Auto-Detected: … (…%)" banner. It was fed by a hardcoded setTimeout — see the product findings below.
  • The Next.js dev badge appears in every capture unless hidden. It is suppressed with injected CSS (nextjs-portal{display:none}) rather than by changing app config.

S2 — how do you get sharp phone footage into a 1080p frame?

The brief offered three routes and predicted the first would be "likely soft" and the third "crispest, heavier". Both predictions turned out to be incomplete:

Route 1 — record at 540x960, upscale

Soft, as predicted. The brief's premise that recordVideo ignores deviceScaleFactor is true — verified: 540x960 stayed 540x960 at dsf 2.

Route 3 — screenshot loop

Unusable. Measured 1.9 fps at dsf 3, because a full-page 1170x2532 PNG takes ~500 ms to encode. A 1.9 fps take cannot show a tap, a keystroke or a scroll. capture/probe-video-scale.mjs keeps the raw evidence.

What actually works: --force-device-scale-factor

The context deviceScaleFactor never reaches the encoder, but Chromium's --force-device-scale-factor flag does. A 540x960 viewport with that flag gives a genuine 1080x1920 capture at a full 25 fps, which is then downscaled 0.563x into the 608x1080 panel. Two controls confirm this is real resolution rather than an upscale: enlarging recordVideo.size alone produces soft edges and colour fringing, while the flag produces clean text at the same output size.

Frame rate. Playwright's recorder emits 25 fps; the deliverable must be exactly 30. Concatenating segments also leaves a non-standard rate in the container, so the final pass re-encodes at CFR 30 (37263360/1242619 → 30/1).

One more discovery that shaped the whole look: ffmpeg -vf drawtext fails on this machine's build — "Cannot load default config file", no fontconfig. Every glyph in the video is therefore rasterised by Chromium. That is a constraint, but it is also why the captions are crisp and why no font files need to ship.

Decision D7

Which staged photo, and why

Five real photographs were sourced from Wikimedia Commons — no stock-photo sites, because searches for "Philippines garbage dump" there return many AI-generated or AI-upscaled results, and generated imagery is out of scope. Each candidate was run twice through the real pipeline: POST /api/v1/reports/triage (COCO YOLO + the waste model + Roboflow, EXIF stripped server-side first), then POST /api/v1/analyze-hazard for law and agency.

The choice rule was a clean, stable result twice in a row — not a fallback message. That rule turned out to be nearly impossible to satisfy, because the triage endpoint was silently broken. See the product findings; the numbers below are from the fixed endpoint.

Why marilao-03.jpg

It is CC0, so the finished video carries no attribution obligation. Its subject matches the story: waste in a river, which is what scenes 1 and 2 describe. Roboflow detects PET at 0.857 on it — the strongest single score of any candidate — and it clears the 0.40 auto-dismiss gate at 0.5811.

Why the narration names no material

Every candidate returns a Vehicle / Traffic indicator — the COCO model reacting to a boat in the water. On the chosen photo the waste model's class is e-waste, not plastic. The scene therefore says only that the AI flags what it recognises, and that a person confirms. Naming "plastic" would describe something the screen did not show.

The nearest miss is worth recording

talisay-cebu.jpg returns the accurate label plastic and would have made a stronger scene, but it is CC BY-SA and shows a dump rather than a waterway. If the owner wants the more accurate label on screen, swapping the photo and the narration is a fifteen-minute change — both are driven from steps.json and assets/photos/sources.json.

Product findings

What the recording work exposed about the product

Building the AVP meant running the real pipeline repeatedly and reading its output. Four things surfaced that are worth more than the video itself. None of them were invented by the AVP work, and all of them affect real users.

1. The citizen-facing AI triage never worked shipped bug · fixed

apps/ai-service/routers/reports.py called analyze_base64_async(stripped_b64, confidence=0.35), but the function's parameter is named confidence_threshold. The call raised TypeError on every request, and a bare except Exception: result = {} swallowed it.

Effect: POST /api/v1/reports/triage answered success: true, has_concern: false, indicators: [], confidence: 0.0 for every photo, in every environment, including production. The pre-submission check told every citizen their photo showed nothing, and the intended 0.35 threshold was never applied.

Against a real waste photo, on the local serviceBeforeAfter
Resulthas_concern=false, indicators=0, confidence=0.0indicators=2, confidence=0.5782
Labels—Vehicle / Traffic; e-waste (severity critical)

Fixed on fix/triage-confidence-arg with a regression test suite that pins the contract: the async wrapper accepts the keyword the router uses, the sync and async signatures agree, the router source cannot reintroduce the bad keyword, a real result maps through to the response, and an inference failure still degrades to a safe 200. Full suite: 96 passed, 2 failed; both failures are test_e2e_migration.py::TestAuthSync and are pre-existing — verified by reverting the file and reproducing them identically.

2. A "critical" detection does not count as a concern

has_environmental_concern requires an indicator whose type is in high_severity_types and whose severity is high or critical. That set is {solid_waste, sanitation, open_burning, air_pollution, water_pollution, habitat_destruction} — it omits hazardous_waste, which is exactly the type the waste model assigns to e-waste, mapped to severity critical.

So a photo that returns a critical hazardous-waste indicator still reports has_concern: false. This is a tuning question rather than a crash, so it was left alone and reported. It is also why the C1 screen keys off the indicators themselves rather than the concern flag — otherwise the UI would have shown "no concern" next to a critical detection.

3. The triage gate is hard to clear by design

composite = max(coco, waste, roboflow) × 0.6 + agreement × 0.3 + sources × 0.1, with ≥ 0.70 auto-routed, 0.40–0.69 pending review, and < 0.40 auto-dismissed. A single source therefore needs ≥ 0.50, or two sources must agree. A real photograph of a waste-filled river scored 0.3914 — dismissed by 0.009.

4. Two mismatches around the offline queue

  • The service worker intercepts POST /api/reports, but the report page submits to /api/v1/ai/reports. The two queues are disjoint, so the app queue never receives what the service worker would catch.
  • Independently, the queue screen never left its loading state under the fixture stack — getAllQueued() did not resolve. This is why scene 6 was cut rather than faked. The brief already flagged that Playwright's offline switch does not cover service workers and that a real-phone test is still required; that test remains outstanding.

5. A confidence percentage is on screen in the admin ticket grid

Every card in apps/admin-portal/…/tickets/page.tsx renders ConfidenceTierBadge with showScore, which prints an AI confidence percentage. Confidence percentages are on the owner's banned list, so scene 5 deliberately uses the triage Kanban and the LGU performance table instead — neither shows one. This is a scene-composition workaround, not a product change; the badge is still there for real users.

6. The canned Liksi reply is legally inaccurate

When the AI gateway is unreachable, apps/mobile-pwa/src/app/api/v1/ai/chat/route.ts returns a hardcoded reply. It was reachable during recording only as a fallback, and it states that open burning and illegal dumping carry fines "from ₱300 up to ₱1,000,000" with "1 to 15 days community service". Per RA 9003 §48, the ₱1,000,000 ceiling belongs to the landfill offences in §48(14)–(16); open burning carries a fine or imprisonment, not community service. Any citizen who hits that fallback is told the wrong exposure. This is a product-content fix, not an AVP fix, and it needs a lawyer's eye rather than an agent's.

Separately, the chat panel is labelled "Statutory Legal AI • Online". Nothing in the product qualifies that framing, and the fallback shows how thin it can be.

7. Ghost Mode's privacy copy and the report flow's third parties

The report screen says "100% private • EXIF-stripped & zero user identifiers". The take evidence shows the capture coordinates (10.6969, 122.5644) being sent to the public OpenStreetMap Nominatim geocoder, alongside Unsplash and Carto map requests. EXIF stripping is real and verified in code (report/page.tsx:392, ai-service/services/exif.py), but "zero user identifiers" sits uneasily next to GPS coordinates leaving for a third-party service. Worth a legal/privacy read before this line is repeated to a government agency.

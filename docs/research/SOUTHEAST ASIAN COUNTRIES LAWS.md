**ENVIRONMENTAL LAWS, ENVIRONMENTAL CULTURE, ENVIRONMENTAL STATUS AND CHALLENGES AND, ENVIRONMENTAL DO’S AND DON’TS**

1\. **BRUNEI**

**ENVIRONMENTAL LAWS (NATIONAL LAWS AND REGULATION)**

**Prevention of Pollution of the Sea Order 2005 \-** aims to prevent disposal of waste from ships, including refuse, garbage, effluents, plastics, and dangerous pollutants. Section 5(1) states that ‘… if any disposal or discharge of refuse, garbage, waste matter, trade effluent, plastics or marine pollutant in packaged form occurs from any ship into Brunei Darussalam waters, the master, the owner and the agent of the ship shall each be guilty of an offence …’. Section 16(1) states that any direct or indirect discharge from ships of refuse, garbage, waste, plastics, effluents, and dangerous pollutants into any part of the sea or waters in Brunei Darussalam shall be charged for recovery costs.

**Prevention of Pollution of the Sea (Garbage) Regulations 2008** \- especially Section 4(1), specifies plastic discharge prohibition, covering all plastics, including but not limited to synthetic ropes, synthetic fishing nets, and plastic garbage bags.

**Law of Brunei Chapter 30 Minor Offences Act 1929 \-** prohibits littering in public. The more recent Miscellaneous Offences Act Chapter 30 of 2023 builds upon the former with some modifications on the penalties for convicted offenders and distinguishes the penalties for individuals and corporations.

**Environmental Protection and Management Act 2022** \- specifically Section 41(1), authorises the minister to make regulations that are necessary or expedient to carry out the order’s provisions. Section 41(2)(f) states that ‘… the Minister may also impose requirements with respect to control of land pollution, including industrial waste, domestic waste and littering’. 

**Hazardous Waste (Control of Export, Import and Transit) Order 2013** \- prohibits the transboundary movement of plastic waste unless the person importing or exporting is authorised to do so.

**Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) Guidelines(2012)** \- A set of rules requiring companies and developers to study and report the possible environmental effects of projects before construction or operation begins.

**Prevention of Pollution Reporting Incidents Regulations(2008) \-** Requires industries, ships, and operators to immediately report pollution incidents such as oil spills or chemical leaks to authorities.

**Environmental Protection and Management Order (EPMO) (2006) \-** Covers the protection of air, water, and land quality, as well as the management of waste and pollution control. Provides a legal basis for establishing environmental standards and pollution limits in various sectors such as industry, transport, and agriculture.

**Town and Country Planning Order (2001) \-** regulates land use and urban planning in Brunei to ensure sustainable development while minimizing environmental degradation.

**National Forestry Policy (2007)** \- Promotes the protection of forest ecosystems, including tropical rainforests, which are crucial for biodiversity and carbon sequestration.

**Wildlife Protection Act (1984)** \-  Is designed to conserve and protect wildlife species in Brunei, particularly endangered species and those facing habitat loss.

**Water Act (2002)** \- regulates the management and protection of water resources in Brunei, ensuring sustainable use and preventing contamination.

**Air Quality Control Regulations (2000) \-** These regulations set standards for air quality and emissions, aiming to reduce pollution and protect public health in Brunei.

**National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (2016)** \- Focuses on protecting Brunei’s rich biodiversity, particularly in the rainforest and marine ecosystems.

**ENVIRONMENTAL CULTURE OF BRUNEI**

The environmental culture of Brunei Darussalam is uniquely defined by state-driven conservation, strict Islamic environmental ethics, and an economic paradox. Unlike many of its Southeast Asian neighbors, Brunei has managed to preserve its dense, pristine rainforests, which cover roughly 81% of its total land area.

**1\. The Core Ideology: Melayu Islam Beraja (MIB)**

Brunei’s national philosophy—Malay Islamic Monarchy (MIB)—heavily influences how citizens interact with nature.

**Khalifah (Stewardship):** Environmental accountability is treated as a deep moral and religious obligation rather than just a legal rule. Islamic teachings dictate that humans are stewards of the Earth (Khalifah) and are answerable to Allah for its preservation.

**Harm Prevention:** The Islamic legal principle of preventing harm (La darar wa la dirar) forms the cultural and regulatory basis for protecting public resources like water systems and pristine marine life.

**2\. The "Resource Curse" Paradox**

**Oil-Financed Conservation:** Ironically, Brunei's environment has been preserved largely due to its massive crude oil and natural gas wealth. Because the economy relies almost entirely on offshore oil revenues, the country never faced the economic pressure to clear its tropical forests for mass agriculture, logging, or commercial palm oil plantations.

**The High Carbon Footprint:** While its primary forests remain structurally untouched, the local lifestyle is highly carbon-intensive. Culturally, car ownership is a major part of daily life due to heavily subsidized fuel prices, leading to exceptionally high per-capita carbon emissions for a small population.

**3\. Deep Pride in Pristine Ecosystems**

**The Green Jewel of Borneo:** Bruneians take intense national pride in keeping their natural landscapes wild and untouched. The crown jewel of this pride is the Ulu Temburong National Park, a dense primary rainforest protected under strict "no-cut" policies. Cultural eco-tourism here is designed around low-impact, highly regulated canopy walks.

**The "Heart of Borneo" Commitment:** Brunei is culturally and politically dedicated to the transnational Heart of Borneo initiative, legally anchoring conservation as a vital part of its \[national economic diversification strategy.

**4\. The Water Village Cultural Struggle (Kampong Ayer)**

**Historic River Connection:** Kampong Ayer is the historic, sprawling stilt-house water village on the Brunei River. For centuries, the river was culturally viewed as a self-cleaning entity that could flush away waste naturally.

**The Plastic Awakening:** With the modern influx of non-biodegradable single-use plastics, the river village faced significant pollution issues. This triggered a major shift in community culture. Today, extensive state-funded and school-led volunteer cleanups ("Care and Love for Environment" programs) are standard, reshaping how the community manages waste.

**5\. Government-Led Environmental Habits**

Because of the absolute monarchical system, environmental behavioral changes are often successfully driven from the top down:

**No Plastic Bag Weekends:** What started as a government campaign has normalized a lifestyle where major retail stores do not provide plastic bags, encouraging a widespread reusable bag culture.

**Bandar Ku Ceria (Happy City):** Every Sunday, the capital city of Bandar Seri Begawan closes its main streets to motor vehicles. The population culturally embraces this day for walking, cycling, and reducing city emissions.

**ENVIRONMENTAL STATUS OF BRUNEI**

Brunei maintains an exceptionally well-preserved natural status on land, but it faces severe structural environmental challenges due to its fossil-fuel-dependent economy and high climate vulnerability. 

While the country is celebrated for its lush, untouched rainforests, it simultaneously grapples with high per capita carbon emissions and escalating climate-induced weather anomalies.

**Current Environmental Status**

**Pristine Forest Cover:** Approximately 72.1% of Brunei's land remains covered by rich tropical rainforests, making it an ecological leader in Southeast Asia.

**High Marine and Land Biodiversity:** The country actively protects fragile ecosystems, managing expansive peat swamp forests and coastal mangrove tracts. Its marine waters host healthy, biodiverse coral reefs, further supported by the strategic deployment of over 1,400 artificial reefs to boost fish stocks and coral propagation. 

**Minimal Agricultural Deforestation:** Unlike its regional neighbors, Brunei has avoided massive deforestation driven by commercial palm oil production or industrial logging, largely because its economy is sustained by offshore oil reserves.

**Key Environmental Challenges**

**1\. Extreme Carbon Footprint and Fuel Dependency** 

**Highest Per Capita Emissions in ASEAN:** Fueled by heavy domestic subsidies, high private vehicle ownership, and energy-intensive lifestyles, Brunei emits roughly 18.28 tons of per capita. 

**Electricity Powered by Fossil Fuels:** Over 99% of the nation's domestic electricity is generated directly by oil and gas. The energy sector remains the country's single largest domestic polluter. 

**2\. Rising Climate Vulnerability** 

**Aggressive Warming Trend:** The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) notes that Brunei's local temperature increases by 0.25°C per decade, while annual rainfall intensifies by 100mm per decade. 

**Frequent Flash Floods and Landslides:** Rapidly intensifying monsoon cycles cause widespread, severe flash flooding and landslides, particularly affecting low-lying urban areas and coastal districts like Belait and Tutong. 

**Destructive Forest and Peatland Fires:** During dry seasons, extreme heat waves ignite massive forest fires. These fires threaten irreplaceable peatlands, which serve as critical global carbon sinks, and generate hazardous seasonal air pollution (haze). 

**3\. Urbanization and Localized Habitat Loss** 

**Infrastructure Encroachment:** While the inland jungles are heavily guarded under the international "Heart of Borneo" treaty, coastal and urban land development—such as large-scale public housing projects—has caused localized primary forest fragmentation and habitat loss.

**National Action and Targets**

To tackle these challenges while protecting its economy, the government operates under the Brunei Darussalam National Climate Change Policy (BNCCP), targeting specific milestones by 2035: 

**Renewable Shift:** Scaling solar power generation up to 30% of total domestic capacity.

**Clean Transportation:** Increasing the market share of electric vehicles to 60% of all annual vehicle sales.

**Carbon Sequestration:** Moving to plant 500,000 new trees to naturally absorb domestic carbon emissions.

**ENVIRONMENTAL DO’S AND DON’TS**

**Environmental DO's**

**DO stick strictly to marked trails:** If you are visiting eco-tourism hubs like Ulu Temburong National Park or the Pulau Selirong Mangrove Reserve, stay on the designated wooden boardwalks. This protects the highly fragile, waterlogged peat soils and root systems from trampling. 

**DO register and accept a guide:** Certain remote nature reserves require you to register at a security checkpoint. You will be legally required to be escorted by an official forestry ranger to ensure safety near international borders and protect the habitat. 

**DO bring your own reusable bags:** The government runs strict national anti-plastic initiatives (such as the "No Plastic Bag" campaign). Many grocery stores and establishments do not provide plastic carriers. 

**DO properly dispose of waste on the water:** If you take a boat tour through the Kampong Ayer water village or coastal mangrove tracks, keep all trash secure inside the vessel. Maritime authorities enforce tight crackdowns on any debris entering the water system.

**Environmental DON'Ts**

**DON'T litter anywhere in public:** Brunei takes public cleanliness incredibly seriously. Under the Miscellaneous Offences Act, dropping trash or throwing rubbish from a vehicle can land individuals an immediate compound fine of up to BND 1,000 (\~USD 740), with repeat offenders facing jail time. 

**DON'T engage in open burning:** Starting outdoor fires to burn garden waste, plastic, or camp garbage is strictly illegal under national land pollution laws. The country fiercely protects its surrounding air quality and vulnerable peat forests from seasonal haze. 

**DON'T disturb, feed, or harvest wildlife:** The Wildlife Protection Act heavily guards the nation's rich biodiversity. It is illegal to catch, harm, or take home any native flora or fauna—including rare hornbills, proboscis monkeys, or marine coral. 

**DON'T fish in Marine Protected Areas (MPAs):** If you are diving or boating, ensure you are not inside a designated marine reserve. The Fisheries Department enforces harsh legal penalties for unauthorized fishing or anchoring that damages localized coral propagation projects.

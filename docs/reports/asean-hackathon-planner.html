👉 "How is this different from existing environmental reporting apps?"
KAT (30s)
Most apps are digital tip jars — they just forward the message. LikasLens is a reasoning system. It doesn't just forward reports; it analyzes the photo, identifies which law was broken, figures out which office enforces it, and routes it automatically. It's the difference between mailing a letter and having an AI lawyer sort it for you.
JEFF (30s)
Three differentiators: (1) Neuro-symbolic — YOLOv8 detects the hazard, Neo4j Cypher traversal connects it to specific laws (PD-705, RA-9003) and agencies (DENR, EMB). (2) Ghost Mode — zero-knowledge architecture, EXIF stripped client-side. (3) Offline-first PWA — IndexedDB queue survives network loss. No other reporting platform combines all three.
👉 "How do you prevent false reports or abuse?"
KAT (30s)
The same way any reporting system does — but we have layers. The AI screens each report. If it doesn't detect a real hazard, the report is flagged. Every report that passes AI still needs a human LGU officer to verify before any action is taken. A single false report can't cause harm.
JEFF (30s)
Three layers: (1) IP-based rate limiting (10/min), (2) AI confidence threshold — reports below 0.40 are auto-dismissed, 0.40-0.69 go to pending_review, only ≥0.70 are auto_routed, (3) mandatory human verification — LGU officers confirm in person before enforcement. We also have a 5m anti-Sybil geofence that rejects duplicate submissions from the same spot within 24h.
⚠ Potential trap: "What about coordinated false-report campaigns?" — Jeff: "We track by IP cluster and report velocity. Pattern escalation controller detects 5+ reports in 2km within 72h. Future: trust-scoring layer (Phase 2)."
👉 "How do you protect whistleblowers in countries where environmental activism is dangerous?"
KAT (30s)
Ghost Mode is our answer. The Philippines is the second-deadliest country for environmental defenders — 57 activists killed since 2020. Ghost Mode strips every piece of identifying data from the report before it leaves the phone: GPS, device ID, timestamp. Even our servers can't link the report to the person. The evidence is real; the reporter is invisible.
JEFF (30s)
Defense-in-depth: (1) Client-side EXIF stripping via canvas re-encode — ≤50ms, strips GPS/device/timestamp. (2) Ghost Mode omits user_id from the payload entirely — we map to an ANONYMOUS_GHOST user on the server. (3) Server-side EXIF strip as fallback via GD/Imagick. (4) No IP logging for ghost reports. (5) Barangay-centroid GPS replacement in development to replace exact coordinates with area-level data.
⚠ Potential trap: "Can't the government just subpoena your server logs?" — Kat/Jeff: "Ghost reports have no user_id, no device fingerprint, no IP log. There is nothing to subpoena. This is zero-knowledge by design."
👉 "What about the fish corral incident? How do you prevent cultural misclassification?"
KAT (30s)
We learned this the hard way during testing in Iloilo. The AI flagged traditional bamboo fish traps as illegal structures. The LGU officer told us: "Those are baklad. That's how we feed our children." That moment changed our design. Now, community verification is a first-class signal, not a downstream filter.
JEFF (30s)
Design response: (1) Reports near known traditional fishing zones are flagged for expedited LGU consultation before graph routing. (2) Our dual-layer human oversight — AI classifies but cannot initiate enforcement. (3) KPI scorecard now requires ≥2 GPS-diverse reports / 500m before escalation. (4) Post-hackathon: cultural co-design consultations for ancestral domain deployment guidelines.
⚠ Potential trap: "Seems like your AI isn't ready for deployment then." — Jeff: "That's exactly why we designed human-in-the-loop from the start. The fish corral incident proved our architecture is correct — the AI flags, humans decide. We never claimed the AI replaces human judgment."

👉 "How does the neuro-symbolic pipeline actually work?"
KAT (30s)
Think of it as a two-brain system. The visual brain (YOLO) looks at the photo and says "that's illegal dumping." The legal brain (Neo4j graph) cross-references what it sees against a database of environmental laws and says "that violates RA-9003, enforced by DENR Region VI." Then Gemini writes a plain-English summary for the agency.
JEFF (30s)
Step by step: (1) Image upload → EXIF strip. (2) YOLOv8n inference (COCO + optional waste model) produces detections with confidence scores. (3) Composite scoring: max(coco_score, env_score) * 0.7 + agreement * 0.3. (4) HazardAnalyzer runs hybrid retrieval — Neo4j Cypher traversal from HazardType through VIOLATES edge to Law, then through ENFORCED_BY edge to Agency. (5) Vector embedding fallback via Gemini text-embedding-004 if graph path fails. (6) Gemini 2.5 Flash generates a grounded report using ONLY retrieved legal context — anti-hallucination enforced. (7) Triage disposition: auto_routed (≥0.70), pending_review (0.40-0.69), auto_dismissed (<0.40).
👉 "What happens when the AI service goes down?"
KAT (30s)
The report still saves. The system degrades gracefully. If the AI is down, reports queue in the database and get processed when the AI comes back. No citizen's report is ever lost because of a server issue.
JEFF (30s)
Graceful degradation: Frontend → Backend reports are stored immediately in PostgreSQL with status=pending_triage. The AI service is called asynchronously. If it's down, the report sits in the queue. When AI recovers, pending triage jobs process automatically. The submission pipeline never blocks on AI availability. We can also kill the AI mid-demo to prove it — the report saves successfully, and we show "pending triage" state on the dashboard.
👉 "Why Neo4j instead of a relational database for the legal graph?"
JEFF (30s)
Environmental routing is a graph problem: hazard → law → agency → jurisdiction → NGO. In SQL, tracing this requires 4-5 JOINs with recursive CTEs. Neo4j Cypher does it in one query: MATCH (h:HazardType)-[:VIOLATES]->(l:Law)-[:ENFORCED_BY]->(a:Agency). That's 3ms vs 50ms+ in PostgreSQL. Plus, adding a new ASEAN country means adding vertices and edges — no schema migrations, no JOIN rewrites. The graph is naturally extensible for transboundary routing.
KAT (30s)
It's also our ASEAN scalability bet. Adding a new country's laws is adding nodes to a graph — not redesigning a database schema. We go from PH to 6 countries by adding data, not rewriting code.
👉 "How does offline mode work?"
JEFF (30s)
Three-tier caching in the service worker: Cache-First for static assets (pages, images), Stale-While-Revalidate for legal reference data (laws, NGOs), Network-First for dynamic data. Reports queue in IndexedDB with a dedicated likaslens-offline store. Background Sync API triggers auto-sync when connectivity returns. We also have a frontend-side queue with localStorage fallback. Both use the same backend API so no data conflicts on sync. We can demo this live: kill WiFi, submit a report, restore WiFi, watch it sync.
KAT (30s)
This matters because ASEAN field connectivity is unreliable. Fisherfolk, farmers, forest rangers — they don't have 5G. The PWA works regardless. Reports never get lost.

The pipeline architecture supports dual-model detection — COCO + environmental — and the composite scoring is designed to accept new models without rewiring.
KAT (30s)
Our hackathon bet was on the integration — proving that neuro-symbolic routing works end-to-end. The model upgrade is a data problem, not an architecture problem. We have the pipeline ready for a custom model the moment our dataset is curated.

👉 "How do you handle false positives from the AI?"

**`REGIONAL RESILIENCE GOALS`**

`When discussing "regional resilience goals," the frameworks are split into two major layers: the overarching ASEAN regional goals (coordinating Southeast Asia as a whole) and the specific national policies of the Philippines applied to its domestic administrative regions.`

`Both frameworks aim to transition Southeast Asia from simply reacting to disasters to actively mitigating, surviving, and recovering from long-term climate and economic shocks.`

## **`1. The ASEAN Regional Resilience Goals`**

`At the Southeast Asian regional level, resilience goals are legally bound by the ASEAN Agreement on Disaster Management and Emergency Response (AADMER) and the ASEAN Climate Vision.`

### **`A. The "One ASEAN, One Response" Mandate`**

* **`The Goal:`** `To establish a unified, cross-border system where member states can instantly share asset pools, early-warning meteorological data, and medical networks during super-typhoons, earthquakes, or severe transboundary haze episodes.`  
* **`Action Plan:`** `Shifting regional financing from reactive humanitarian aid to ex-ante (pre-disaster) risk insurance pooling through programs like the ASEAN Disaster Risk Finance and Insurance (ADRFI) scheme.`

### **`B. Regional Plan of Action for Adaptation to Drought (ARPA-AD)`**

* **`The Goal:`** `To protect the agriculture sector—which employs over 110 million people across the ASEAN footprint—from intensifying El Niño cycles and long-term water scarcity.`  
* **`Action Plan:`** `Harmonizing data sharing via the ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre to implement uniform soil moisture tracking and a regional early-warning drought indicator index.`

## **`2. Philippine Regional Resilience Goals`**

`Domestically, the Philippine government localizes resilience targets through its Philippine Development Plan (PDP) and the Resilient and Green Human Settlements Framework (RGHSF).`

### **`A. Implementing Risk-Informed Local Planning`**

* **`The Goal:`** `Eliminating fragmented data by mandating all Local Government Units (LGUs) to use centralized, science-based hazard maps to update their local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plans (LDRRMPs).`  
* **`Action Plan:`** `Forcing integration with digital architectures like GeoRiskPH and the PlanSmart application. This ensures that when a region plans new housing, infrastructure, or zoning laws, it uses data modeling to avoid high-risk fault lines, landslide zones, and areas prone to severe storm surges.`

### **`B. Ecosystem-Based Adaptation (Ridge-to-Reef Management)`**

* **`The Goal:`** `Enhancing natural buffers to protect local economies, infrastructure, and food supply chains.`  
* **`Action Plan:`** `Moving away from building simple concrete seawalls toward holistic "Ridge-to-Reef" management. This involves massive upland reforestation to stop soil erosion, coupled with the restoration of coastal mangrove forests and coral reefs to naturally absorb typhoon storm surges.`

### **`C. Elevating Local Financial Resiliency and Fund Utilization`**

* **`The Goal:`** `Resolving the historical bottleneck where emergency funds sit unused at the municipal level due to bureaucratic confusion.`  
* **`Action Plan:`** `Standardizing the Local Resilience Readiness Monitoring Framework (LRRMF) to audit and guide local governments on how to efficiently access, clear, and deploy their local disaster funds before a hazard escalates into an unmanageable humanitarian crisis.`
